Features

Hot Corner

Quickly move the pointer into the top-left corner of any screen to open Mission Control, complete with a GNOME-style ripple animation.

Close Windows in Mission Control

Hold the pointer over a window thumbnail to reveal a close button, just as in GNOME’s Activities overview.

Tile Assist

After you tile a window to the left or right half of the screen, a list of other windows appears in the remaining space, ready to fill the gap.

Green Button Fills the Screen

The green button expands the window to fill the screen instead of entering full screen. Click it again to return to the previous size.

Dark Menu Bar

When a window fills the screen, a black bar appears behind the transparent menu bar, keeping it readable.

Caps Lock Indicator

A centered indicator appears near the bottom of the screen whenever you turn Caps Lock on or off.

Scroll to Switch Spaces

Scroll over the Apple menu to move a display to the next or previous space, just as scrolling over the top bar does in GNOME.

Space Indicator

A row of dots shows which space you’re viewing whenever a display switches spaces—whether you used the keyboard, Mission Control, a swipe, or a scroll over the Apple menu.

Keyboard and Mouse

Press ⌥

Press the Option key to open or close Mission Control.

Press ⌥ Twice

Press Option twice to open the apps window in Spotlight.

⌥1 through ⌥9

Open apps in the Dock by number. You can choose which number opens the Finder.

Type to Search in Mission Control

Start typing, as you would in GNOME’s Activities overview, and Mission Control closes, handing what you typed to Spotlight. You can choose where the search opens: Spotlight or the apps window.

Window Tiling

Press Option and an arrow key to tile a window to the left or right half of the screen, fill the screen, or return to the previous size.

Cut and Paste Files in the Finder

Cut, then paste in the Finder to move files instead of duplicating them. Use Command-X and Command-V, or Control-X and Control-V.

Home and End Keys

The Home and End keys move the insertion point to the beginning and end of the current line, as they do on Linux.

Menu Key

The Menu key on PC keyboards opens a shortcut menu, even in apps that don’t support it natively.

Middle-Click Paste

Middle-click a text field to paste at the spot where you click (X11-style). If text is selected, the selection is pasted—like the X11 primary selection—instead of the contents of the Clipboard. Middle-click a Dock app icon to open a new window (or to open an app that isn’t running). Middle clicks everywhere else pass through unchanged, preserving native browser behavior, such as opening links in a new tab.

Click-Through Focus

One click on an inactive window both brings it forward and passes through to what’s under the pointer, so pausing a video in a background window no longer takes two clicks. It works across apps and between windows of the same app.

Scroll Zoom

Control-scroll in a browser to zoom in and out, with either natural or traditional scrolling.

More Features

Available in 11 Languages

English, Arabic, Chinese (Simplified), French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese (Brazil), Russian, and Spanish.

Fine-Grained Control

Every feature can be turned on or off in SuperOpt’s settings, down to individual keyboard shortcuts.

Excluded Apps

Add the apps you want left alone. While you’re using one of them, every feature is off and the menu bar icon dims, so the app behaves exactly as it would without SuperOpt.

Fully Offline and Open Source

No telemetry, no network requests, no data collection. Everything runs locally on your Mac. The full source is on GitHub.

Settings

Install

Install with Homebrew:

brew install --cask logonoff/bucket/superopt

Or download the latest release directly from GitHub Releases, then open the app. Builds are self-signed but not notarized. The first launch may be blocked. If it is, open System Settings, click Privacy & Security, scroll down, then click Open Anyway. Alternatively, remove the quarantine attribute with xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/SuperOpt.app.

Permissions

SuperOpt requires Accessibility and Input Monitoring permissions in Privacy & Security settings. The app asks for these permissions the first time you open it.

Warning

This project was largely generated by AI, and the code has not been thoroughly vetted by a human. Use at your own risk.

Verification

Every release carries a GitHub artifact attestation that links the build to its source commit and workflow run, confirming that the binary was built from the published source code and hasn’t been tampered with. Verify with gh attestation verify SuperOpt.zip --repo logonoff/superopt.

Releases are immutable, the Homebrew cask pins a SHA-256 hash, and all commits are GPG-signed.

The code-signing public key is also available.

Shortcut Remapping

Maps Linux keyboard shortcuts to their macOS equivalents—copy, cut, paste, and undo (Control-C, Control-X, Control-V, and Control-Z), browser shortcuts, terminal shortcuts, and more.
